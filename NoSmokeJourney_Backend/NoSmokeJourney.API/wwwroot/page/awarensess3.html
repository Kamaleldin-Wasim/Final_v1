Myths vs. Facts

Let's separate truth from fiction. Learn the real facts about smoking, vaping, and lung health.

Debunking Common Misconceptions

Misinformation about smoking and lung cancer can be dangerous. Here are the facts you need to know, backed by science.

❌ Myth

"Only smokers get lung cancer."

✅ Fact

While smoking is the #1 risk factor, 10-20% of lung cancer cases occur in people who have never smoked. Causes include radon gas, air pollution, secondhand smoke, and genetic factors.

Source: American Cancer Society
❌ Myth

"Light cigarettes are safer than regular ones."

✅ Fact

There is no safe cigarette. "Light" or "low-tar" versions still cause lung cancer, heart disease, and COPD. Many smokers compensate by inhaling deeper or smoking more frequently.

Source: WHO, CDC
❌ Myth

"The damage is already done — quitting won't help now."

✅ Fact

It's never too late. Your body begins to repair itself within hours of your last cigarette. After 10 years, lung cancer risk is cut in half. Quitting at any age significantly lowers your risk.

Source: American Lung Association
❌ Myth

"Vaping is a completely safe alternative to smoking."

✅ Fact

E-cigarettes still contain nicotine and harmful chemicals like heavy metals and cancer-causing compounds. Long-term risks are still being studied, but vaping is not "safe".

Source: FDA, National Institutes of Health
❌ Myth

"Lung cancer is a death sentence."

✅ Fact

Treatment has improved dramatically. Immunotherapy and targeted therapies are saving lives. When caught early (Stage I), the 5-year survival rate is 90%. Early detection is key.

Source: National Cancer Institute
❌ Myth

"Secondhand smoke isn't dangerous."

✅ Fact

Secondhand smoke causes thousands of deaths annually. Non-smokers exposed to secondhand smoke have a 20-30% higher risk of lung cancer. Everyone deserves clean air.

Source: CDC, Surgeon General
❌ Myth

"Quitting smoking makes you gain too much weight."

✅ Fact

Some people gain a small amount of weight (average 4-7 lbs), but this is far less harmful than continuing to smoke. Regular exercise and healthy eating can manage any weight change.

Source: Mayo Clinic
❌ Myth

"Smoking helps me relax and manage stress."

✅ Fact

Nicotine actually increases anxiety and stress over time. The "relaxation" you feel is simply relief from nicotine withdrawal. True stress relief comes from exercise, sleep, and healthy coping strategies.

Source: British Medical Journal
