Understanding the Issue

What Smoking Does to Your Body

Smoking affects nearly every organ in the body. Each cigarette contains over 7,000 chemicals, hundreds of which are toxic and about 70 of which can cause cancer.

The good news? Your body begins healing almost immediately after you stop smoking. Within months, your lung function improves significantly — and your risk of heart disease drops by half within just one year.

What Happens When You Quit

Your body starts recovering the moment you put out that last cigarette.

⏱️

20 Minutes

Heart rate and blood pressure begin to drop toward normal levels.

🌬️

12 Hours

Carbon monoxide level in your blood drops to normal — more oxygen reaches your organs.

🫀

2–12 Weeks

Circulation improves and lung function increases noticeably.

🌱

1–9 Months

Coughing and shortness of breath decrease. Cilia regrow in the lungs.

🎉

1 Year

Risk of coronary heart disease is cut in half compared to a smoker.

🏆

10–15 Years

Risk of lung cancer drops to about half that of a smoker. Life expectancy returns to near-normal.

Gentle Steps Toward a Smoke-Free Life

Change doesn't happen overnight — and that's okay. Here's how to begin.

01
🎯

Set a Quit Date

Pick a meaningful date within the next two weeks. Having a target makes it real.

02
🤝

Build Your Support Circle

Tell friends and family. Having people who cheer for you makes a big difference.

03
🧘

Find Your Triggers

Stress, coffee, social situations — knowing your triggers helps you prepare coping strategies.

04
🏃

Move Your Body

Even a short daily walk reduces cravings and improves lung capacity over time.
